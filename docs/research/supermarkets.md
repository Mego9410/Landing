## 1. Executive summary

1. **No major UK grocer has a public API for adding items to a basket in October 2026.** Tesco ran a public developer programme from about 2009 to the late 2010s ([Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/analysis/1844786/tesco-door-developers); [The Grocer](https://thegrocer.co.uk/news/tesco-gives-app-developers-access-to-product-information/544428.article)). A 2026 developer write-up says it "was retired years ago and nothing has replaced it" ([DEV Community, 2026](https://dev.to/yappman/how-to-get-tesco-prices-with-an-api-in-2026-4n20)). No public replacement was found for Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Waitrose, Ocado, Co-op or Iceland.
2. **Partner access does exist, but you only get it through a deal.** Whisk, now Samsung Food, had basket integrations with Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Ocado and Waitrose ([FoodNavigator, 26 Jan 2021](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2021/01/26/Tech-firm-Whisk-lands-Sainsbury-s-as-retailers-double-down-on-online-shopping-trend)). Cherrypick (formerly Lollipop) builds baskets for Sainsbury's and Tesco ([The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/shopping-platform-cherrypick-to-introduce-paid-for-tiers/692727.article)). The Guardian and Ocado made every Feast recipe shoppable into an Ocado basket ([Campaign](https://www.campaignlive.co.uk/article/chop-shop-guardian-partners-ocado-weekly-recipe-experience/1721101)). These are bilateral commercial agreements, not self-serve APIs.
3. **Tesco now competes directly with Landing's core feature.** In April 2026 Tesco began a trial of an AI assistant inside its app, built with OpenAI and Tomoro AI. It plans meals and adds the ingredients to the Tesco basket. About 280,000 colleagues got it first, and a customer rollout is due "later this year" ([Grocery Gazette, 9 Apr 2026](https://www.grocerygazette.co.uk/2026/04/09/tesco-trials-ai-shopping-assistant-with-280000-colleagues-ahead-of-customer-rollout/)). This affects how keen Tesco will be to partner.
4. **The quickest way to real basket fill-up for several retailers is an aggregator.** Pepesto sells a pay-as-you-go API that turns a recipe into a matched cart for Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons and Waitrose, priced at about €0.32 per one-shot call and €1.20 per session of up to 50 items on the starter tier ([Pepesto pricing, via search](https://www.pepesto.com/pricing/); [pepesto-mcp README](https://github.com/pepesto-solutions/pepesto-mcp)). The user finishes checkout in **Pepesto's** app or hosted UI, so Landing would not own the final step, and how Pepesto reaches each retailer is not documented publicly.
5. **Only Amazon has an official, documented way to fill a multi-item basket from a URL.** This is Amazon's Associates "Add to Cart form" ([Amazon PA-API docs](https://webservices.amazon.co.jp/paapi5/documentation/add-to-cart-form.html)). Amazon.co.uk carries Morrisons, Iceland and Co-op groceries ([Retail Gazette, Feb 2026](https://www.retailgazette.co.uk/blog/2026/02/amazon-expands-iceland-grocery-partnership/); [Grocery Gazette, 23 Sep 2025](https://www.grocerygazette.co.uk/2025/09/23/amazon-fresh-close-14-stores/)) and same-day fresh food in parts of London ([About Amazon UK, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/retail/amazon-grocery-same-day-delivery-uk)). PA-API 5 was retired on 30 April / 15 May 2026, and the Creators API replaces it ([Amazon](https://affiliate-program.amazon.com/creatorsapi/docs/en-us/paapiv5-deprecation)).
6. **Scraping, or logging in on a user's behalf, is technically possible but high risk.** Open-source tools such as `uk-grocery-cli` and `basketeer` drive real Tesco, Sainsbury's and Ocado accounts using the user's credentials or session cookies. Both say they are unofficial and may breach retailer terms ([uk-grocery-cli](https://github.com/abracadabra50/uk-grocery-cli); [basketeer](https://github.com/tobyandrews1985/basketeer)). A consumer app should not build its business on this.
7. **Recommendation:** launch with no partnerships. Do the meal planning, the aggregated list and the ingredient matching yourself, and hand off with deep links to retailer search or product pages, Amazon multi-item cart links, and list exports. Next, trial an aggregator (Pepesto) for one-tap baskets. Then pursue direct deals with Tesco, Sainsbury's and Ocado, ideally through their retail media teams, once you have users to show them.

### Notes on individual retailers

**Tesco.** The Tesco Labs API was launched for app developers around 2009–2010. It offered product search and basket access in its early form, and later only product data ([ITPro](https://itpro.com/607668/online-grocery-shopping-gets-the-tesco-api-treatment); [The Grocer](https://thegrocer.co.uk/news/tesco-gives-app-developers-access-to-product-information/544428.article)). npm and GitHub clients still point to `devportal.tescolabs.com` ([tesco-api-node](https://github.com/ImClarky/tesco-api-node)). A 2026 developer article says the programme "was retired years ago and nothing has replaced it" ([DEV, 2026](https://dev.to/yappman/how-to-get-tesco-prices-with-an-api-in-2026-4n20)). **I could not find an official Tesco shutdown notice. Treat the API as dead.** Tesco's own recipe site (Tesco Real Food) has had a "Shop ingredients / buy ingredients" button that adds a recipe to the logged-in user's basket, with a "you probably already have these" section ([Econsultancy](https://econsultancy.com/how-do-waitrose-and-tesco-use-on-site-content-marketing/)). Tesco has also powered shoppable recipes for third parties: more than 5,000 BBC Good Food recipes and MyDish.co.uk ([InternetRetailing](https://internetretailing.net/?p=29606)). Tesco therefore *has* partner-grade basket endpoints. They are not public, and Tesco now runs its own AI meal planner (see section 1, point 3). The unofficial `basketeer` SDK shows the current web app talks to a GraphQL gateway behind Akamai bot protection, and sign-in needs a real browser ([basketeer](https://github.com/tobyandrews1985/basketeer)). That is a sign Tesco actively blocks automated access.

**Sainsbury's.** It has had two meal-planner partners with real basket and slot integration: Whisk (2021) and Lollipop (2022), which became Cherrypick ([The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/meal-planning-and-shopping-platform-lollipop-rebrands-as-cherrypick/687537.article)). This is the clearest UK precedent for a deal like the one Landing wants. Cherrypick reported more than 330,000 users and has added paid tiers ([FoodNavigator, 27 Jun 2024](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2024/06/27/Cherrypick-A-new-recipe-box-competitor/)).

**Ocado.** Ocado Retail (the consumer business) and Ocado Group/Solutions (the Ocado Smart Platform technology licensed to Kroger, Sobeys, Coles, Aeon, Morrisons and others) are separate businesses ([Ocado Group newsroom](https://www.ocadogroup.com/newsroom/news/ocado-welcomes-new-partner-aeon)). The **Ocado Smart Platform is fulfilment and e-commerce infrastructure for retailers, not an API for consumer apps**. Kroger and Sobeys have reportedly scaled back their commitments. For Landing, the useful route into Ocado is Ocado Retail's media and partnerships team, which has run whole-recipe shopping campaigns. The Guardian Feast campaign reached 14 million people, and 89% of product-page visits came from non-Ocado customers ([Media Week Awards entry](https://mediaweekawards.co.uk/finalists/one-step-recipe-shopping-with-ocado-g0028)). That is a customer-acquisition story Landing can offer too.

**Waitrose (and the Ocado history).** Waitrose groceries were sold through Ocado from 2000 to September 2020. M&S then replaced Waitrose on Ocado through a £750m 50% joint venture ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/2020/08/will-your-ocado-shopping-be-cheaper-or-more-expensive-when-marks-and-spencer-products-replace-waitrose/); [techround](https://techround.co.uk/business/ocado-will-become-marks-and-spencers-in-september-2020/)). Waitrose.com is now in-house. Recipes on waitrose.com are a direct competitor to Landing.

**Morrisons.** This is the most multi-channel grocer. Morrisons.com runs on Ocado technology, increasingly with in-store picking. Morrisons has sold on Amazon since 2016 and also sells through Deliveroo, Uber Eats and Just Eat ([Yorkshire Post](https://www.yorkshirepost.co.uk/business/consumer/how-you-can-now-do-your-weekly-morrisons-shop-amazon-2946263)). **Morrisons-on-Amazon is the one big-supermarket range that Landing could fill into a basket today through an official mechanism**, Amazon's cart URL. Prime and location limits apply.

**Aldi and Lidl.** They have no online grocery shop to send a basket to. Aldi's Deliveroo pilot only began this month in 8 stores, and Deliveroo has no public multi-item basket link. For these users, Landing should export a list (print, Reminders or share sheet), ideally sorted into a typical Aldi or Lidl store layout. Prices can only be estimated, for example from Aldi's website product pages or third-party price data.

## 5. Product matching: recipe ingredient to SKU

**The problem.** "200 g chicken breast", "1 tin chickpeas" and "a handful of coriander" have to become a specific pack at a specific retailer, with a quantity, a price and an own-brand-or-branded choice. The plan must also combine across recipes: three recipes using 150 g, 200 g and 250 g of chicken should become 600 g, rounded up to packs, with leftovers flagged.

**Recommended pipeline (build in-house; this is Landing's competitive advantage):**

1. **Parse** each ingredient line into quantity, unit, canonical ingredient, preparation and optional flag. Use a rules-based parser plus an LLM fallback, and cache by line text. Normalise to metric (g/ml/each) with a density table for volume-to-weight conversion (cups or tbsp of flour, for example).
2. **Canonical ingredient taxonomy** of about 1,500 to 3,000 nodes, such as `chickpeas > canned > drained`. Each recipe ingredient links to one node, not to a SKU.
3. **Per-retailer SKU candidates for each node.** Each candidate holds pack size, unit price (per kg/l, now standardised under the reformed Price Marking Order; see below), tier (value, own-brand, premium or branded), dietary attributes and GTIN where known.
4. **Pack optimisation:** choose the pack combination that covers the aggregated need for the week at the lowest cost, applying user preferences (cheapest, own-brand, organic). Track leftovers, such as "the other half of the coriander", and suggest recipes that use them up.
5. **Substitution graph** for out-of-stock items and diets, e.g. chicken thigh for breast, or a vegan swap.
6. **Pantry staples:** default to "you probably have this" for salt, oil and spices, following Tesco Real Food's pattern ([Econsultancy](https://econsultancy.com/how-do-waitrose-and-tesco-use-on-site-content-marketing/)).

**Data sources:**

- **GTIN/EAN barcodes** are the most reliable way to join data across retailers for branded products. Own-brand products have retailer-specific GTINs, so cross-retailer matching for own-brand items has to be done by attributes (category, size, tier).
- **Open Food Facts:** an open, crowd-sourced product database. The project says "the complete database is published as open data and can be reused by anyone and for any use" ([openfoodfacts-server README](https://github.com/openfoodfacts/openfoodfacts-server)). The database licence is ODbL, which requires attribution and share-alike for derived databases. Images are CC-BY-SA, and the API asks for a custom User-Agent and has rate limits. **[ODbL and rate-limit details from background knowledge, UNVERIFIED in this session.]** UK coverage is partial and has **no prices**. Use it for nutrition, allergens and barcodes.
- **GS1 UK:** the official GTIN registry. "Verified by GS1" offers basic attribute lookup. Full product content (images, nutrition) comes through GDSN data pools and syndicators. **[UNVERIFIED pricing and access terms, search quota exhausted.]**
- **Price data:**
  - **Trolley.co.uk** compares Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Waitrose, Iceland and others. In 2022 it nearly closed when its data broker demanded a £28,800 per year licence. Trolley **licenses** scraped feeds rather than scraping itself ([Grocery Gazette](https://www.grocerygazette.co.uk/?p=33843); [MoneySavingExpert, Jun 2022](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/news/2022/06/supermarket-comparison-site-could-be-forced-to-close-down-due-to/)). This gives a **realistic price benchmark for a licensed UK multi-retailer price feed: tens of thousands of pounds a year.** Trolley has no public API that I could find.
  - **Pepesto** offers daily-refreshed prices as part of its API ([search summary](https://www.pepesto.com/supermarkets/tesco-gb/)).
  - **ONS:** used web-scraped Tesco, Sainsbury's and Waitrose prices from 2014 ([ONS blog](https://digitalblog.ons.gov.uk/2016/06/27/what-we-can-do-with-prices-data-scraped-from-the-web)) and **added grocery scanner data (about 50% of the market) to CPI from March 2026** ([ONS methodology](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/methodologies/introducinggroceryscannerdataintoconsumerpricestatistics)). These are aggregated indices, **not SKU-level prices you could use in the app**.
  - **Which?** publishes a monthly cheapest-supermarket basket comparison ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/reviews/supermarkets/article/supermarket-price-comparison-aPpYp9j1MFin)). **The Grocer 33** is a weekly 33-item basket ([The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/the-grocer-33/price-cut-promotions-help-sainsburys-win-cheapest-supermarket/715552.article)). Both are useful for "about £X per week" marketing claims, not for SKU prices.
- **Keeping prices fresh:** show prices as "approx., checked {date}". Refresh the top 5,000 SKUs daily and the long tail weekly from whichever licensed feed you use. Always treat the retailer's page as the final price, and label loyalty prices (Clubcard, Nectar) separately. That labelling is now mandatory in-store under the PMO reform and good practice in-app.

**Regulatory note on prices.** The CMA pushed for clearer unit pricing, including on loyalty prices. The reformed Price Marking Order applies from **6 April 2026** (postponed from 1 Oct 2025). It requires per-kg/per-litre units and separate unit prices for loyalty and standard prices ([Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/new-laws-to-make-supermarket-pricing-clearer-thanks-to-which-amawr4p4KBti); [Herrington Carmichael](https://herrington-carmichael.com/insights/commercial-law/are-you-compliant-with-the-price-marking-order-reforms/)). The PMO applies to traders, not comparison apps. Still, if Landing displays or compares prices, the CMA's consumer-protection expectations for comparison tools and the **DMCC Act 2024** unfair-practices regime require accurate, up-to-date prices and clear disclosure of paid rankings or commercial relationships, e.g. "sponsored match". **[DMCC applicability is a general legal view; take advice.]**

## 7. Legal and risk analysis (UK)

**This is not legal advice. Get a short opinion from a UK tech lawyer before Phase 2.**

1. **Scraping public pages (prices, catalogue).**
   - *Contract:* retailer website terms almost always prohibit robots, scraping and data mining. Breach exposes you to an injunction or damages ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/is-web-scraping-legal-in-the-uk/)). Tesco's actual terms could not be fetched **[UNVERIFIED wording]**.
   - *Database right:* the Copyright and Rights in Databases Regulations 1997 protect databases that took substantial investment, and repeated extraction of insubstantial parts can add up to infringement ([EM Law, Oct 2023](https://emlaw.co.uk/data-scraping-navigating-the-challenges-old-and-new-part-2-intellectual-property/)). A supermarket's product and price catalogue is a strong candidate. The Trolley.co.uk dispute shows **data brokers enforce licences on scraped grocery data** ([MoneySavingExpert](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/news/2022/06/supermarket-comparison-site-could-be-forced-to-close-down-due-to/)).
   - *Copyright/TDM:* the UK has no commercial text-and-data-mining exception. CDPA s.29A covers non-commercial research only, and reform consultation was still in progress in 2026 ([search summary](https://law.ungovr.org/ai/gb)).
   - *Technical:* Tesco sits behind Akamai and blocks datacentre IPs ([basketeer](https://github.com/tobyandrews1985/basketeer); [Apify](https://apify.com/piotrv1001/tesco-groceries-scraper.md)). Getting round anti-bot measures raises the risk under the Computer Misuse Act.
   - **Risk: medium to high.** Prefer a licensed feed or an aggregator that takes on this risk contractually (ask for indemnities).
2. **Logging in on users' behalf (credentials, cookies, session tokens).**
   - *Computer Misuse Act 1990 s.1:* unauthorised access to computer material. Access "authorised" by the account holder but forbidden by the system owner is a grey area, and bypassing controls such as CAPTCHAs or bot detection makes it worse ([Sprintlaw](https://sprintlaw.co.uk/articles/what-is-scraping-uk-legal-overview/)).
   - Users would breach their own customer terms by sharing credentials, and accounts or Clubcards could be suspended.
   - *UK GDPR:* storing retailer credentials and session tokens means high-risk processing and security duties. A breach would expose order history, addresses and payment-adjacent data. You would need a DPIA.
   - Apple App Review may reject apps that collect third-party credentials without authorisation from the service **[general knowledge]**.
   - **Risk: high. Do not do this**, even though open-source tools show it is technically possible ([uk-grocery-cli](https://github.com/abracadabra50/uk-grocery-cli)).
3. **Deep linking to public search or product URLs:** low risk. This is ordinary hyperlinking. Do not frame retailer pages deceptively or use their logos without permission; use plain text names, or logos only under partner brand guidelines.
4. **Affiliate links:** low risk, but follow ASA/CAP rules on disclosing affiliate links ("we may earn a commission") and each network's terms. Some forbid use in apps, or cashback or incentivised clicks.
5. **GDPR for account linking (Phase 3 OAuth-style partner integrations):** use the retailer's OAuth if one is offered. Store tokens encrypted (for example in Vercel environment variables plus a KMS-encrypted column), state the purpose in the privacy notice, and set out data-sharing roles with the retailer. Usually each party is an independent controller, and the agreement should cover this.
6. **Price comparison claims:** if Landing shows "cheapest at X", it must be accurate and current. DMCC Act 2024 consumer enforcement by the CMA has applied directly since April 2025 **[general knowledge]**. Disclose sponsored placements.
7. **Platform risk:** retailers change web front-ends without notice (`uk-grocery-cli` warns of exactly this), and Tesco's own AI planner reduces its incentive to support third-party basket builders.

## 8. Recommendation: phased plan

### Phase 1: launch with no partnerships (about 8–12 weeks of engineering)

**What ships:**
- Supermarket picker covering Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Waitrose, Ocado, Iceland, Co-op, Amazon, plus "Aldi / Lidl / other (in-store)".
- Meal plan builder and an **aggregated weekly list**: unit normalisation, pack rounding, a pantry "already have" list and leftover tracking.
- Ingredient matching to a **canonical taxonomy** (no SKUs yet), with retailer-agnostic search terms per node, e.g. `chickpeas` maps to "chickpeas in water 400g".
- **Hand-off per retailer:** a checklist "shopping mode" that opens each item's search URL in SFSafariViewController or the retailer's app through a universal link, with tick-off state kept in Landing.
- **Amazon:** a one-tap multi-item cart link for Morrisons, Iceland and Co-op on Amazon and Amazon grocery, using Creators API ASIN lookups. Join Amazon Associates UK.
- Exports: share sheet, clipboard, Apple Reminders, printable list grouped by aisle (generic aisle map, plus Aldi and Lidl layouts).
- Approximate weekly cost using a **coarse price model** (median price per canonical ingredient per retailer tier, refreshed monthly by hand or from a cheap licensed sample), labelled "estimate".
- Apply to the **Awin programmes** for Tesco, Morrisons and Iceland where open. Search links can carry affiliate tracking: Awin deep links wrap any landing URL, so tracked search-page links should work **[UNVERIFIED per programme; check whether the programme allows app traffic]**.

**Cost:** engineering only. Amazon Associates and Awin publisher accounts are free to join (Awin has historically charged a small refundable publisher deposit **[UNVERIFIED]**).
**Risks:** a clunky hand-off (many taps) for the big four grocers; affiliate income will be small, about £1–£6 per new customer.

### Phase 2: aggregator for one-tap baskets (about 4–8 weeks, plus due diligence)

- **Pilot Pepesto** (or any similar provider that emerges) for Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons and Waitrose. Unit cost is about €1.20 per weekly basket session on the starter tier ([pricing via search](https://www.pepesto.com/pricing/)). At 10,000 weekly baskets that is about €12k a month, so it needs a revenue model: subscriptions, affiliate income, or the free `predirect` mode where the user pays in Pepesto's app.
- **Due diligence checklist:** written confirmation of how each retailer is accessed (authorised partner, or automation of user accounts); an indemnity; uptime SLA; data-protection agreement; who holds the end-customer relationship; whether checkout through Pepesto's app is acceptable under Apple guidelines and good for your UX.
- Alternatively, or as well, **license a SKU and price feed** for in-house matching (the Trolley case suggests about £25–30k a year for a multi-retailer feed). Ask for indemnities against database-right claims.
- Ask **Samsung Food B2B** whether its shoppable-recipe API is still sold to third parties **[UNVERIFIED]**.

### Phase 3: direct retailer deals (6–18 months, business development)

- **Order of approach:** (1) **Ocado Retail / Ocado Ads**, which is publisher-friendly and has recipe-to-basket precedent; (2) **Sainsbury's**, which has done it twice (Whisk, Lollipop/Cherrypick); (3) **Tesco**, the biggest prize but now building its own AI planner, so approach dunnhumby with an offsite-media angle; (4) Asda and Morrisons.
- **What you need first:** about 50,000–100,000 monthly active planners, evidence of incremental new-to-retailer customers (copy the Guardian/Ocado 89% non-customer metric), and SOC2-style security answers.
- **Deal shapes:** a basket API or white-label "add recipe to trolley" endpoint plus a CPA or revenue share; retail-media sponsored ingredient matches that brands pay for; Clubcard or Nectar co-marketing.
- **Costs:** BD time, legal review of contracts (about £5–15k), security assessments. There is no public pricing.

### What to avoid
- Storing users' supermarket passwords or session cookies, or running headless browsers against retailer sites to fill baskets.
- Making scraped price feeds a dependency for core functionality without a licence.
